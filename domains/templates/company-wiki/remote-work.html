Working remotely, well

Updated last week · 5 min read

We're a remote-first team spread across several time zones. That only works if we're deliberate about it: we write things down, we treat asynchronous as the default, and we protect each other's focus time.

Async by default

Most work doesn't need a meeting. Write a short doc, share it, and let people respond when their day allows. A clear message that someone can read in their own time zone beats a call that forces half the team to join at an awkward hour. Save real-time for the things that genuinely need it — kickoffs, hard decisions, and getting to know each other.

Meetings that earn their place

If we do meet, there's an agenda beforehand and notes afterward, posted where everyone can find them. No agenda, no meeting. We record the important ones so nobody has to choose between sleep and being in the room, and decisions always get written up so they outlive the call.

Rule of thumb: If a decision isn't written down somewhere searchable, it didn't happen. Capture it, then move on.
